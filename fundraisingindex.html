Whose Money Is It

All funds a team raises belong to Virginia Valor FC, the 501(c)(3) — not to the team or to individual families. We track what each team raises and it is spent only on that team's expenses, but it is held and disbursed by the club.

Approval

Every fundraising idea needs club approval in writing before it goes public. Nothing gets promoted, posted, or announced to families until Dylan has signed off on the plan.

Branding

The Valor logo is club property. Branded gear runs through the club's supplier — if a team wants logo'd items, it goes through the office. Some items can be approved case-by-case.

Approved Payment Methods

  • Your team's Square link or QR code
  • A club Square reader at an event
  • Cash — counted and turned in within 48 hours
  • Check payable to Virginia Valor FC only — never a person or team name

Never use a personal Square account, Venmo, Cash App, PayPal, or Zelle — for the club's sake and yours (a personal account can end up with a 1099-K for club money).

Processing Fees

  • Square link or QR code: 3.3% + $0.30
  • Club reader at an event: 2.6% + $0.15
  • Keyed in by hand: 3.5% + $0.15
  • Cash and check: no fee

How This Page Fits In

This portal is where you track what your team has raised and ask to use it. It is a record and a request form — it does not hold, move, or release money on its own.

Approving a request here is the club saying yes to the expense. The club then pays the vendor or reimburses whoever fronted the cost, following the normal process. Your team's official balance lives in the club's monthly financial reports, and Dylan reconciles this page against those.

How Funds Can Be Used

Yes: tournament and showcase registration, travel including hotel and van rental, indoor and futsal fees, field and referee costs, approved team gear, and coach travel for that team's events.

No: refunds, credit toward one player's individual fees, cash at season end, or a balance that follows a player who leaves.

Team fundraising money is never split or tracked per player. Federal rules for nonprofits prohibit tying a financial benefit to how much a specific family raised. If a balance is applied to fees, it has to reduce every player's fee equally. Anything not clearly on this list — check with the office first.

Tax Deductibility and Sponsors

Do not tell anyone a purchase or contribution is tax deductible unless the club has confirmed that in writing for that specific fundraiser. Someone buying doughnuts did not make a donation.

Sponsor recognition is fine (name, logo, a thank you, a link). Advertising is not — discount codes or ad-style promotion can turn it into taxable income for the club.

Raffles

Raffles and drawings have to clear the office first. Virginia regulates charitable gaming.

Not sure about something? Check with Dylan before you launch a new fundraiser — it is much easier to sort out up front than after money has changed hands.
