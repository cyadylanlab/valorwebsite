The rules.

These are the 2026 playing rules. Read them with your team before tournament day, especially the goal box and the extra-player rule when a game runs away.

Download the rules as a PDF

Check in 30 minutes early.

Report to the game-site headquarters before your first match. Have proof of age on hand for every player. Medical releases are signed online ahead of time, so there is nothing else to hand over.

01 Rosters and player eligibility
  • Teams need at least 3 players and no more than 6. Four or five is a typical roster.
  • Register in the division of your oldest player. For 2026-27 age groups run August 1 to July 31, not by calendar birth year, so check the table above before you enter.
  • A player born in September 2016 is U10, and a player born in June 2016 is U11, even though both were born in the same calendar year. Players born between August and December are the ones most likely to have moved.
  • Divisions may be combined if entries are light in any bracket.
  • Girls' divisions are reserved for girls. Girls may also play in boys' divisions.
  • Every player needs proof of age on hand at the tournament. We accept a current club player card, or a copy or photo of a birth certificate or passport. Paper or on a phone both work. Show it whenever a tournament official asks.
02 Equipment and uniforms
  • Shin guards are mandatory for every player, no exceptions.
  • Any soccer-appropriate footwear is allowed unless stated otherwise. Indoor venues may restrict cleats.
  • Game balls are provided. U8 through U12 use a size 4 ball, U13 and U14 use a size 5.
  • Wear matching shirts. Jersey numbers are optional.
  • Bring two contrasting shirt colors so teams can resolve color conflicts.
03 Field, goal box and scoring
  • Fields measure approximately 24 to 30 yards by 16 to 20 yards. Dimensions may vary with the venue.
  • The goal box measures 8 feet across and 4 feet deep. Players can move through it but cannot play the ball inside it.
  • A defender touching the ball in the box gives the opponent a goal. An attacker touching it in the box gives the defending team a goal kick.
  • A ball that comes to rest in the box results in a goal kick for the defending team.
  • A scoring shot must be taken from the attacking half.
04 Match format and substitutions
  • Play two 12-minute halves with a 2-minute halftime.
  • One-day events provide at least four games, potentially six or seven with playoffs. Multi-day formats provide at least six, potentially eight or more with playoffs.
  • Substitute during a stoppage with the referee's permission.
  • Pool matches can finish tied. Playoff matches require a winner.
  • Blowout rule. A team that falls six goals behind may add a fourth player. Once the margin comes back to five, that extra player comes off. There is no mercy rule and no early finish, every game plays its full two halves.
05 Restarts and special playing rules
  • No offside. Sliding is prohibited and results in an indirect free kick.
  • Heading is permitted in U11 and older divisions.
  • Defenders stay at least 10 feet from the ball at dead-ball restarts.
  • Restart within five seconds, subject to referee discretion.
  • Use kick-ins in place of throw-ins. Restarts are indirect except for corner kicks and penalties.
  • Take goal kicks from anywhere on the end line, outside the goal box. Kickoffs may travel in any direction.
  • Take penalties from the center spot, with all other players behind midfield. A missed penalty does not remain live.
06 Standings, tiebreakers and playoffs

Pool standings award 3 points for a win, 1 for a tie and 0 for a loss. A forfeit is recorded as a 5-0 loss.

Resolve standings ties in this order

  1. Head-to-head result, when exactly two teams are tied.
  2. Goal difference.
  3. Fewest goals allowed.
  4. Most goals scored.
  5. A system-generated coin toss.

Tied playoff matches

Play one three-minute golden-goal period. If no one scores, the three players who finished the game take part in a penalty shootout.

07 Conduct and tournament authority
  • Referees can issue cards to players or coaches. A red-carded player leaves the current match and is suspended for the next one.
  • The Tournament Director may remove players, coaches or teams for misconduct. When the responsible coach cannot be identified, the head coach is sanctioned.
  • Referee abuse is not tolerated. Unsporting conduct can lead to removal or disqualification of teams, coaches or spectators.
  • The Tournament Director decides situations these rules do not address.
08 Tournament-day check-in
  • Submit your roster on the Cup roster page before the roster deadline. Rosters are not entered in PlayMetrics. PlayMetrics is for registration and payment only.
  • Every player needs a Cup medical release, signed online by a parent or guardian before the deadline. Once the roster is submitted, the coach gets a team link to send to families. Club and generic release forms are not accepted.
  • Check in at your game-site headquarters 30 minutes before the first match. We check you in from the roster you submitted, and jersey numbers are not required.
  • Have proof of age on hand for every player: a current club player card, or a copy or photo of a birth certificate or passport. Show it if a tournament official asks.

Common questions.

These are the questions we got most in year one.

Does my child have to play for a club?

No. Plenty of teams are a group of friends, a school class or a rec team playing together for the day. Club players are welcome too.

What if we only have three players?

Three is enough to enter. It does mean nobody gets a rest, so most teams bring four or five.

My child changed age groups this year. Why?

Youth soccer moved from calendar birth year to the school year for 2026-27, so age groups now run August 1 to July 31. Players born between August and December are the ones who shifted. Use the table near the top of this page, or Valor's age group transition page, and go by the date of birth rather than the school grade.

Can my team play up an age group?

Yes. Your age group is set by your oldest player, so you can always enter older, never younger. Organizers may also combine divisions if entries are light.

Can girls play in a boys' division?

Yes. Girls may play in either division. Boys may only play in the boys' divisions.

Who coaches the team on the day?

A parent or any adult from your group. You do not need a licensed coach. The adult who submits the roster is who we contact about schedules and field assignments.

What happens if the weather is bad?

The tournament runs in the cold and light rain. If conditions become unsafe, the Tournament Director makes the call and every registered contact gets an email and a text.
